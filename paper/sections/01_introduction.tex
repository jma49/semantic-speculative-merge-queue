\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Continuous Integration (CI) guards mainline branches against regressions and build breakages~\cite{hilton2016usage,zhao2017impact,bernardo2023impact,wessel2023github}. At high submission rates---amplified by automated and agent-assisted pull-request (PR) workflows---serial verification bottlenecks delivery: a $12$~min suite caps serial throughput at $5$~PR/h with zero overhead regardless of runner capacity. Engineering teams therefore adopt batched, prioritized, or speculative queues such as Google's TAP~\cite{elbaum2014techniques,memon2017taming}, Uber's SubmitQueue~\cite{ananthanarayanan2019keeping}, Meta's lean CI~\cite{juloori2025scale}, Bors, GitHub Merge Queue, GitLab Merge Trains, and PR-prioritizing pipelines~\cite{fallahzadeh2023accelerating,jungwirth2025improving}.

While build and test selection shorten individual jobs~\cite{leong2019assessing,machalica2019predictive,jin2023hybridcisave,schwendner2025practical}, arrival-ordered (FIFO) batching still dominates hosted queues, and speculative outcome trees expend compute on branches whose coupled edits fail together~\cite{ananthanarayanan2019keeping}. Auditing 600 strictly consecutive default-branch merges across \texttt{kubernetes/kubernetes}, \texttt{microsoft/TypeScript}, and \texttt{vercel/next.js} (Section~\ref{sec:background}), we find a two-level directory collision in \emph{every 8-PR FIFO window} (\TraceFifoOverlapPct{}\%), even though disjoint 4-PR subsets exist in \TraceNextDisjPct{}\% of rolling windows on \texttt{next.js} and \TraceKbsDisjPct{}\% on \texttt{kubernetes}. Co-scheduling coupled PRs invites \emph{semantic merge conflicts} that pass individually yet fail together~\cite{zhang2022trained,svyatkovskiy2022program}, while \emph{flaky tests}~\cite{luo2014empirical,bell2018laker,lam2019idflakies,lam2020large,parry2025systemic} force a trade-off between clean-PR false rejections and costly batch reruns~\cite{henderson2023flake,najafi2019bisecting}.

Conditional on a clean base commit $M_0$ with no candidate-to-base persistent conflicts (otherwise an additional cross-base survival factor applies), independent PR defects $q_i$, pairwise intra-batch conflicts $P(c_{ij})$ over coupled edges $E(B)$, and a false-failure-only flake channel $\phi$, the first-attempt pass probability of batch $B$ ($K=|B|$) is:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{pass} \mid B, M_0) = (1 - \phi) \prod_{i \in B}(1 - q_i) \prod_{(i,j) \in E(B)}(1 - P(c_{ij})).
\label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
Unconstrained $E(B)$ lets colliding pairs grow quadratically in $K$, eroding $P(\text{pass}\mid B, M_0)$ and wasting runner capacity.

We design, formalize, and evaluate \textbf{SSMQ (Semantic Speculative Merge Queue)}, targeting Eq.~\eqref{eq:survival} through three mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Graph-Disjoint Risk-Stratified Batching} eliminates observed edges $\hat{E}(B)$. Using a warm-up dependency graph $\hat{G}$ and P90 risk scales learned strictly from prior commits, SSMQ packs cone-disjoint low-risk PRs into fast lanes ($K \le K_{\max}$) and isolates colliding or high-risk PRs in starvation-bounded protected lanes ($K \le 2$).
    \item \textbf{Two-Regime Beta-Binomial Flaky Gating} estimates suite flakiness online via a plug-in $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$ heuristic with asymmetric cutoffs---guarding singletons before rejection ($\tau_1 = 0.25$) while suppressing batch reruns below $\tau_B = 0.50$.
    \item \textbf{Fail-Safe 1-Step AST/Traceback Suspect Ranking} localizes multi-PR failures via \texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker}, intersecting AST function diffs with specificity-weighted 1-hop test call graphs in \BenchASTMs{}~ms under binary-bisection fallback.
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate SSMQ across three validation layers. First, on \BenchTotalBatches{} executed Python \texttt{unittest} failure batches with 50/50 balanced comment (\BenchCommentBatches{}) and sibling-function (\BenchSiblingBatches{}) distractors, \texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker} attains \BenchASTTopOnePct{}\% Top-1 accuracy (\BenchASTSiblingPct{}\% vs.\ \BenchFileTriviaSiblingPct{}\% on siblings over a nested file ablation) and \BenchASTLOOPct{}\% leave-one-out recovery. Second, across 10 exact-Git-tree replays running live \texttt{git merge}, \texttt{unittest}, and \texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker}, SSMQ cuts CI runs by \GitReplayCISavePct{}\% and bisection splits by \GitReplaySplitSavePct{}\% (\TotalEscapedDefects{} escapes). Third, across \TotalSealedRuns{} SHA-256-sealed v5 suites (\TotalSealedRecords{} replay records, \TotalEscapedDefects{} modeled escapes), SSMQ cuts survivor P95 latency by \LatRedVsStaticPct{}\% vs.\ static FIFO (\SSMQPNinetyFiveLat{} vs.\ \StaticPNinetyFiveLat{}~min, Holm $p\HolmPLatVsStatic{}$) and \LatRedVsDepTreePct{}\% vs.\ a bridge-safe dependency-aware outcome-tree proxy (\DepTreePNinetyFiveLat{}~min, $p\HolmPLatVsDepTree{}$). Cone-disjoint packing drives macro gains---holding under uncorrelated uniform defects (\UncorrRecEightySSMQ{} vs.\ \UncorrRecEightyStatic{}~min) and reversing cleanly at $0\%$ recall (\RecallZeroStaticPNinetyFive{} vs.\ \RecallZeroSSMQPNinetyFive{}~min) and zero conflict (\NoConfStaticPNinetyFive{} vs.\ \NoConfSSMQPNinetyFive{}~min)---while keeping failed macro roots small (\FacEightKEightFailSz{}~PRs), so 1-step blame shows negligible macro latency delta yet cuts real bisection splits by \GitReplaySplitSavePct{}\% on wider failing batches.

Our contributions are: (1)~an auditable 600-PR consecutive merge study (Section~\ref{sec:background}); (2)~SSMQ's algorithms and scoped safety, dispatch, and depth bounds (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}); (3)~an \BenchTotalBatches{}-batch symmetric blame benchmark and 10-seed real Git-tree replay (Sections~\ref{sec:eval_setup}--\ref{sec:results}, RQ1); and (4)~a sealed multi-trace evaluation with fixed-density recall and zero-correlation controls (RQ2--RQ5).
