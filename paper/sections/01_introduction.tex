\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Continuous Integration (CI) guards mainline branches against functional regressions, build breaks, and integration defects across modern software repositories~\cite{hilton2016usage,zhao2017impact,bernardo2023impact,wessel2023github}. At industrial scale---and increasingly as autonomous coding agents multiply pull-request (PR) submission rates---strictly serial mainline verification saturates quickly: a 12-minute CI suite caps serial throughput at fewer than five merges per hour regardless of available compute runners. Consequently, large engineering organizations rely on batched and speculative merge queues such as Google's TAP~\cite{elbaum2014techniques,memon2017taming}, Uber's SubmitQueue~\cite{ananthanarayanan2019keeping}, Meta's lean merge pipelines~\cite{juloori2025scale}, Bors, GitHub Merge Queue, and GitLab Merge Trains~\cite{fallahzadeh2023accelerating,jungwirth2025improving}.

Despite extensive work on regression test selection~\cite{leong2019assessing,machalica2019predictive,jin2023hybridcisave,schwendner2025practical}, existing merge queue schedulers share a structural blind spot: they group and speculate over queued PRs strictly in arrival (FIFO) order without inspecting repository dependency structure. Our empirical audit of 600 chronologically merged PRs across \texttt{kubernetes/kubernetes}, \texttt{microsoft/TypeScript}, and \texttt{vercel/next.js} (Section~\ref{sec:background}) demonstrates that \emph{every single consecutive 8-PR FIFO window} ($100\%$ across all three repositories) contains at least one two-level module collision, even though mutually disjoint 4-PR batches exist in the majority of rolling windows on large codebases. Packing overlapping PRs in FIFO order exposes batches to pairwise \emph{semantic merge conflicts}---where two PRs pass independently yet break integration tests when merged together~\cite{owhadikareshk2019predicting,zhang2022trained,svyatkovskiy2022program}. Compounded by pervasive non-deterministic test flakiness~\cite{luo2014empirical,bell2018laker,lam2019idflakies,lam2020large,parry2025systemic}, a single collision or flake triggers cascading \emph{speculative invalidation storms}, expensive $O(\log K)$ binary bisection~\cite{najafi2019bisecting,fallahzadeh2023accelerating}, or silent false rejections of innocent PRs.

Formally, consider a candidate merge batch $B$ of size $K=|B|$ with individual PR defect probabilities $q_i$, pairwise semantic conflict probabilities $P(c_{ij})$ over overlapping dependency edges $E(B)$, and transient CI suite flakiness $\phi$. Under conditional independence, the first-attempt green survival probability of batch $B$ is:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{pass} \mid B) = (1 - \phi) \prod_{i \in B}(1 - q_i) \prod_{(i,j) \in E(B)}(1 - P(c_{ij})).
\label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
Conventional FIFO batching and linear speculative trains leave $|E(B)|$ uncontrolled; as $K$ increases, the quadratic conflict factor $\prod_{(i,j)\in E(B)}(1 - P(c_{ij}))$ collapses $P(\text{pass}\mid B)$, turning deep speculation into wasted runner capacity.

We design, formalize, and evaluate \textbf{SSMQ (Semantic Speculative Merge Queue)}, a dependency-aware merge queue scheduler that directly targets all three failure modes in Eq.~\eqref{eq:survival} while enforcing cumulative mainline safety and starvation freedom:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Graph-Disjoint Risk-Stratified Batching}: Constructs a leak-free dependency graph $\hat{G}$ exclusively from chronological warm-up commits (historical file co-change cliques and two-level module neighborhoods) to pack mutually disjoint, low-risk PRs into fast-lane batches ($K \le K_{\max}$) while promoting high-risk or colliding PRs into starvation-bounded protected lanes ($K \le 2$).
    \item \textbf{Two-Regime Beta-Binomial Flaky Gating}: Maintains an online conjugate $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$ posterior over observable suite outcomes and applies asymmetric rerun thresholds---guarding singletons aggressively before irreversible rejection ($\tau_1 = 0.25$) while avoiding wasteful full-batch retries unless posterior flakiness exceeds $\tau_B = 0.50$.
    \item \textbf{Fail-Safe 1-Step AST/Traceback Causal Blame}: On deterministic multi-PR batch failure, invokes a sub-3~ms static AST-diff and traceback call-graph ranker (\texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker}) to evict Top-1 suspect $s_1$ and concurrently verify $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ and $\{s_1\}$, falling back deterministically to $s_2$-prioritized binary bisection on misattribution or multi-culprit failures.
\end{enumerate}

To prevent circular evaluation and overclaiming, we ground our empirical study in both \emph{real execution} and \emph{non-circular trace replay}. First, we build an executable multi-module Python micro-benchmark of \BenchTotalBatches{} real failing multi-PR \texttt{unittest} batches (\BenchSingleBatches{} disjoint regressions, \BenchCofileBatches{} co-file distractor regressions, and \BenchSemBatches{} verified pairwise semantic conflicts) and compare \texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker} against random, churn, and a strong non-AST \texttt{FileTracebackOverlapRanker} baseline. Second, we calibrate a discrete-event simulator over 600 real open-source PRs that decouples the ground-truth conflict oracle $G^*$ from the scheduler's observed graph $\hat{G}$ (sweeping graph recall $\rho \in [40\%, 100\%]$ plus a zero-conflict control), enforces cumulative mainline safety (\TotalEscapedDefects{} escaped defects across \TotalSealedRecords{} sealed runs), benchmarks against a faithful EuroSys~'19 outcome-tree SubmitQueue~\cite{ananthanarayanan2019keeping}, and explicitly maps the failure-density regimes where 1-step blame succeeds versus saturates.

This paper makes four concrete contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Trace Characterization}: Quantitative evidence across 600 merged PRs from Kubernetes, TypeScript, and Next.js documenting $100\%$ FIFO-8 module overlap alongside high disjoint-batch feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:background}).
    \item \textbf{SSMQ Scheduler \& Formal Properties}: Complete algorithms and formal analysis for cumulative mainline safety, protected-lane starvation freedom, and expected recovery complexity (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}).
    \item \textbf{Real Executable Multi-PR Blame Micro-Benchmark}: \BenchTotalBatches{} executed Python failure batches demonstrating \BenchASTTopOnePct{}\% Top-1 (\BenchASTTopTwoPct{}\% Top-2) accuracy in \BenchASTMs{}~ms vs.\ \BenchFileTopOnePct{}\% for file-level traceback overlap (Section~\ref{sec:results}, RQ1).
    \item \textbf{Non-Circular, Reproducible End-to-End Evaluation}: \TotalSealedRuns{} SHA-256-sealed suites (\TotalSealedRecords{} simulation records) with matched paired Wilcoxon + Holm-Bonferroni tests, Cliff's $\delta$ effect sizes, latency--acceptance Pareto analysis, and an open reproducible artifact (Sections~\ref{sec:eval_setup}--\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}
