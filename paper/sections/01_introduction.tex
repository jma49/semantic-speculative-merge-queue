\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Continuous Integration (CI) safeguards mainline branches against functional regressions, build breakages, and integration defects across modern software repositories~\cite{hilton2016usage,zhao2017impact,bernardo2023impact,wessel2023github}. At industrial scale---and increasingly as autonomous coding agents multiply pull-request (PR) submission rates---strictly serial mainline verification becomes an acute bottleneck: a 12-minute CI suite caps serial throughput below five merges per hour regardless of available runner capacity. To sustain developer velocity, engineering organizations rely on batched and speculative merge queues such as Google's TAP~\cite{elbaum2014techniques,memon2017taming}, Uber's SubmitQueue~\cite{ananthanarayanan2019keeping}, Meta's lean merge pipelines~\cite{juloori2025scale}, Bors, GitHub Merge Queue, and GitLab Merge Trains~\cite{fallahzadeh2023accelerating,jungwirth2025improving}.

Despite substantial progress in regression test selection and prioritization~\cite{leong2019assessing,machalica2019predictive,jin2023hybridcisave,schwendner2025practical}, production merge queue schedulers share a fundamental blind spot: they batch and speculate over queued PRs strictly in arrival (FIFO) order without inspecting repository dependency structure. Our empirical trace study of 600 chronologically merged PRs across \texttt{kubernetes/kubernetes}, \texttt{microsoft/TypeScript}, and \texttt{vercel/next.js} (Section~\ref{sec:background}) shows that \emph{every consecutive 8-PR FIFO window} (\TraceFifoOverlapPct{}\% across all three repositories) contains at least one two-level module collision, even though mutually disjoint 4-PR batches exist in most rolling windows of modular codebases. Bundling overlapping PRs in arrival order exposes batches to pairwise \emph{semantic merge conflicts}, where two changes pass pre-submit checks independently yet fail integration tests when combined~\cite{owhadikareshk2019predicting,zhang2022trained,svyatkovskiy2022program}. Compounded by pervasive non-deterministic test flakiness~\cite{luo2014empirical,bell2018laker,lam2019idflakies,lam2020large,parry2025systemic}, a single intra-batch conflict or transient flake triggers cascading \emph{speculative invalidation storms}, multi-round $O(\log K)$ binary bisection~\cite{najafi2019bisecting,fallahzadeh2023accelerating}, or false rejections of clean PRs.

Formally, consider a candidate merge batch $B$ of size $K=|B|$ whose constituent PRs carry individual defect probabilities $q_i$, pairwise semantic conflict probabilities $P(c_{ij})$ across coupled dependency edges $E(B)$, and transient CI suite flakiness $\phi$. Under conditional independence, the first-attempt pass probability of batch $B$ is:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{pass} \mid B) = (1 - \phi) \prod_{i \in B}(1 - q_i) \prod_{(i,j) \in E(B)}(1 - P(c_{ij})).
\label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
Semantics-blind FIFO batching and linear speculative trains leave the edge set $E(B)$ unconstrained. As batch size $K$ grows, the pairwise interaction product $\prod_{(i,j)\in E(B)}(1 - P(c_{ij}))$ degrades $P(\text{pass}\mid B)$ super-linearly, converting deep speculation into discarded runner compute.

We design, formalize, and evaluate \textbf{SSMQ (Semantic Speculative Merge Queue)}, a dependency-aware merge queue scheduler that directly addresses each failure term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:survival} while guaranteeing cumulative mainline safety and starvation freedom:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Graph-Disjoint Risk-Stratified Batching} eliminates observed pairwise edges $E(B)$ in fast-lane batches. Using a leak-free dependency graph $\hat{G}$ learned strictly from chronological warm-up commits (historical file co-change cliques and two-level module cones), SSMQ packs mutually disjoint, low-risk PRs into fast lanes ($K \le K_{\max}$) while routing colliding or high-risk PRs into starvation-bounded protected lanes ($K \le 2$).
    \item \textbf{Two-Regime Beta-Binomial Flaky Gating} mitigates the $(1 - \phi)$ term along the latency--acceptance Pareto frontier. SSMQ tracks suite flakiness online via a conjugate $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$ posterior and enforces asymmetric retry thresholds---aggressively guarding singletons prior to irreversible rejection ($\tau_1 = 0.25$) while suppressing full-batch reruns unless posterior flakiness exceeds $\tau_B = 0.50$.
    \item \textbf{Fail-Safe 1-Step AST/Traceback Causal Blame} accelerates post-failure recovery when individual defects $q_i$ or latent off-graph conflicts strike a multi-PR batch. A deterministic static-analysis ranker (\texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker}) inspects AST function diffs and 1-hop stack-trace call graphs in under 3~ms to evict Top-1 suspect $s_1$ and concurrently verify $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ alongside $\{s_1\}$, falling back deterministically to $s_2$-prioritized binary bisection on multi-culprit or misattributed failures.
\end{enumerate}

To preclude circular evaluation and delineate exact operating boundaries, we combine \emph{real test execution} with \emph{non-circular trace replay}. First, we construct an executable six-module Python benchmark of \BenchTotalBatches{} failing multi-PR \texttt{unittest} batches (\BenchSingleBatches{} disjoint regressions, \BenchCofileBatches{} co-file distractor regressions, and \BenchSemBatches{} verified pairwise semantic conflicts) and benchmark \texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker} against random, churn, and a deterministic \texttt{FileTracebackOverlapRanker} baseline. Second, we replay 600 real open-source PRs in a discrete-event simulator that decouples the ground-truth conflict oracle $G^*$ from the scheduler's observed graph $\hat{G}$ across recall levels $\rho \in [40\%, 100\%]$ and a zero-conflict control ($P_c = 0$), enforces cumulative mainline verification (\TotalEscapedDefects{} escaped defects across \TotalSealedRecords{} sealed runs), and compares against five baselines including a faithful EuroSys~'19 outcome-tree SubmitQueue~\cite{ananthanarayanan2019keeping}. Our ablations and regime sweeps establish four empirical takeaways: (a)~graph-disjoint risk-stratified batching is the dominant driver of latency and compute savings; (b)~two-regime Beta-Binomial gating occupies the knee of the latency--false-rejection Pareto frontier; (c)~1-step AST/traceback blame yields statistically significant speedups in single-culprit regimes ($8\%$--$15\%$ defect rate, $K=8$) before saturating under multi-culprit high-defect batches ($22\%$, $K=12$) exactly as predicted analytically; and (d)~static FIFO batching remains modestly faster on the conflict-free control ($P_c = 0$), confirming non-circularity.

In summary, this paper makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Trace Characterization}: Analysis of 600 chronologically merged PRs from Kubernetes, TypeScript, and Next.js revealing universal (\TraceFifoOverlapPct{}\%) FIFO-8 module collisions alongside widespread non-FIFO disjoint-batch feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:background}).
    \item \textbf{SSMQ Architecture \& Formal Guarantees}: Scheduling algorithms and analytical proofs establishing cumulative mainline safety, protected-lane starvation freedom, and single- vs.\ multi-culprit recovery bounds (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}).
    \item \textbf{Executable Multi-PR Blame Micro-Benchmark}: \BenchTotalBatches{} real failing Python batches where AST/traceback causal ranking attains \BenchASTTopOnePct{}\% Top-1 (\BenchASTTopTwoPct{}\% Top-2) accuracy in \BenchASTMs{}~ms versus \BenchFileTopOnePct{}\% for file-level traceback overlap (Section~\ref{sec:results}, RQ1).
    \item \textbf{Non-Circular, Sealed End-to-End Evaluation}: \TotalSealedRuns{} SHA-256-sealed experimental suites (\TotalSealedRecords{} simulation replays) evaluated with Holm-Bonferroni-adjusted paired Wilcoxon tests, Cliff's $\delta$ effect sizes, Pareto frontier analysis, and an open verification artifact (Sections~\ref{sec:eval_setup}--\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}
