\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

High-velocity software delivery exposes the structural fragility of semantics-blind FIFO merge queues, where universal module collisions and transient test flakiness turn deep speculation into cascading invalidation storms. We presented \textbf{SSMQ (Semantic Speculative Merge Queue)}, unifying leak-free warm-up dependency-graph disjoint batching, two-regime Beta-Binomial flaky gating, and fail-safe AST/traceback 1-step causal blame attribution under a cumulative mainline safety invariant.

Across \BenchTotalBatches{} real executed Python multi-PR failure batches (\BenchASTTopOnePct{}\% Top-1 and \BenchASTTopTwoPct{}\% Top-2 in \BenchASTMs{}~ms, outperforming file-level traceback overlap by over 25 percentage points overall and \BenchASTCofilePct{}\% vs.\ \BenchFileCofilePct{}\% on co-file distractors) and \TotalSealedRuns{} SHA-256-sealed simulation suites totaling \TotalSealedRecords{} replays over 600 real open-source PRs with \TotalEscapedDefects{} cumulative mainline escapes, SSMQ reduces P95 merge latency by \LatRedVsStaticPct{}\% vs.\ static batching ($p\HolmPLatVsStatic{}$), \LatRedVsUberPct{}\% vs.\ EuroSys~'19 outcome-tree SubmitQueue ($p\HolmPLatVsUber{}$), and \LatRedVsFlakePct{}\% vs.\ fixed-retry batching while cutting CI compute costs across all three repositories. These gains persist even when $60\%$ of true semantic conflicts lie outside the scheduler's observed graph, establishing dependency-aware multi-lane scheduling as a practical foundation for next-generation CI merge queues.
