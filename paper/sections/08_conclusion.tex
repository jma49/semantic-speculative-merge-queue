\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Under the two simulated loads, historical cone exclusion made batches smaller and increased both survivor latency and clean false rejection relative to disabling exclusion. The stacked FIFO outcome tree yielded the lowest mean latency in these experiments. Public merge records supply change footprints and departure times; the rates of interference, arrivals, and failures in an operational queue remain unknown. The executable experiments offer a narrower result: function-level traceback evidence can distinguish sibling edits in a shared file, while its additional benefit over file-level evidence is small in the file-disjoint replay. A field evaluation would need queue admissions, tested base revisions, and the outcomes of revalidation to determine which cost dominates in deployment.
