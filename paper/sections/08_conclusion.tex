\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

High-velocity software delivery exposes a core limitation of arrival-ordered FIFO merge queues: pervasive module collisions and non-deterministic test flakiness routinely turn deep speculation into cascading invalidation storms. We introduced \textbf{SSMQ (Semantic Speculative Merge Queue)}, a dependency-aware scheduler that unifies leak-free warm-up graph-disjoint risk batching, two-regime Beta-Binomial flaky gating, and fail-safe 1-step AST/traceback causal blame under a strict cumulative mainline safety invariant.

On \BenchTotalBatches{} real executed Python multi-PR failure batches, SSMQ's static AST/traceback ranker achieves \BenchASTTopOnePct{}\% Top-1 (\BenchASTTopTwoPct{}\% Top-2) accuracy in \BenchASTMs{}~ms, improving over file-level traceback overlap (\BenchFileTopOnePct{}\% overall; \BenchASTCofilePct{}\% vs.\ \BenchFileCofilePct{}\% on co-file distractors) without runtime coverage overhead or LLM inference. Across \TotalSealedRuns{} SHA-256-sealed suites totaling \TotalSealedRecords{} replays of 600 real open-source PRs with \TotalEscapedDefects{} cumulative mainline escapes, SSMQ reduces P95 merge latency by \LatRedVsStaticPct{}\% relative to static FIFO batching ($p\HolmPLatVsStatic{}$), \LatRedVsUberPct{}\% relative to a EuroSys~'19 outcome-tree SubmitQueue ($p\HolmPLatVsUber{}$), and \LatRedVsFlakePct{}\% relative to fixed-retry batching while lowering CI runner consumption on every repository. Our ablations and regime sweeps show that proactive graph-disjoint packing drives the primary speedup---persisting even when $60\%$ of semantic conflicts lie off-graph---while asymmetric Beta-Binomial gating secures the knee of the latency--false-rejection Pareto frontier and 1-step causal blame accelerates single-culprit recovery before gracefully ceding to bisection in multi-culprit regimes.
