\section{SSMQ Architecture, Algorithms, and Formal Properties}
\label{sec:methodology}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the end-to-end architecture of \textbf{SSMQ}. Newly approved PRs enter the ready queue, receive risk scores and 1-hop impact cones derived from a leak-free warm-up dependency graph $\hat{G}$, and are scheduled across $W$ parallel CI workers via risk-stratified disjoint lanes. Passing batches must clear a cumulative mainline conflict check before committing; failing batches pass through a two-regime Beta-Binomial flaky gate and, when diagnosed as deterministic multi-PR failures, invoke static AST/traceback 1-step causal blame with guaranteed fallback to prioritized binary bisection.

\subsection{Leak-Free Warm-Up Dependency Graph and Risk Scoring}
To prevent temporal lookahead leakage, SSMQ builds its observed dependency graph $\hat{G} = (V, \hat{E})$ exclusively from the chronological warm-up prefix (the earliest $25\%$ of merged PRs in each repository). Let $F(p)$ denote the set of file paths modified by PR $p$, and let $\mu(f)$ denote the two-level directory module prefix of file $f$ (e.g., \texttt{pkg/scheduler} or \texttt{tsc/internal}). From the warm-up history, SSMQ extracts two complementary locality signals:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Historical Co-Change Neighborhood} $\mathcal{N}_{\text{cochange}}(f)$: All files co-modified alongside $f$ within prior warm-up PRs, excluding bulk mechanical commits touching more than $25$ files so formatting sweeps do not create dense spurious cliques.
    \item \textbf{Structural Module Neighborhood} $\mathcal{N}_{\text{mod}}(\mu(f))$: All known files residing under the same two-level directory module $\mu(f)$.
\end{itemize}
The observed 1-hop \emph{impact cone} of PR $p$ is the union of its direct edits and both neighborhoods:
\begin{equation}
C(p) = F(p) \cup \bigcup_{f \in F(p)} \Big(\mathcal{N}_{\text{cochange}}(f) \cup \mathcal{N}_{\text{mod}}(\mu(f))\Big).
\label{eq:cone}
\end{equation}
SSMQ also assigns each incoming PR a normalized defect-risk score $r(p) \in [0, 1]$ calibrated solely against warm-up maxima:
\begin{equation}
r(p) = 0.45\,\tilde{\ell}(p) + 0.30\,\tilde{f}(p) + 0.25\,\tilde{c}(p),
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\ell}(p)$ is log-scaled line churn ($\log(1+\text{add}+\text{del})$), $\tilde{f}(p)$ is normalized modified-file count, and $\tilde{c}(p)$ is the maximum warm-up co-change degree centrality over $F(p)$. By design, SSMQ treats $\hat{G}$ as an imperfect heuristic rather than a complete oracle: latent off-graph couplings outside $C(p)$ cannot violate correctness because every batch still undergoes full CI verification and deterministic fault isolation.

\subsection{Graph-Disjoint Risk-Stratified Multi-Lane Packing}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:packing} specifies SSMQ's batch construction policy whenever any of the $W$ CI workers becomes idle while the ready queue $Q$ is non-empty. To guarantee forward progress and prevent indefinite overtaking, SSMQ unconditionally anchors each new batch at the queue head $p_h = Q[0]$. Whenever $p_h$ carries elevated intrinsic risk ($r(p_h) \ge \theta_{\text{risk}} = 0.55$) or was previously bypassed due to an impact-cone collision ($p_h \in \mathcal{P}$), SSMQ opens a \textbf{Protected Lane} batch bounded at size $K_{\text{prot}} = 2$. Otherwise, it opens a \textbf{Fast Lane} batch bounded at $K_{\max} = 8$, scanning subsequent PRs of the matching lane class in arrival order and admitting $p_j$ only when its impact cone is disjoint from the accumulated batch cone ($C(p_j) \cap U = \emptyset$). Any candidate skipped due to cone overlap is immediately recorded in the promotion set $\mathcal{P}$, ensuring bounded wait once it advances to the head of $Q$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Graph-Disjoint Risk-Stratified Batch Packing}
\label{alg:packing}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Ready queue $Q$, observed cones $C(\cdot)$, risk scores $r(\cdot)$, deferred set $\mathcal{P}$, cap $K_{\max}=8$, risk cutoff $\theta_{\text{risk}}=0.55$
\ENSURE Disjoint batch $B$ and updated queue $Q$
\STATE $p_h \leftarrow Q[0]$ \COMMENT{Always include head for starvation freedom}
\STATE $\text{prot} \leftarrow (p_h \in \mathcal{P}) \lor (r(p_h) \ge \theta_{\text{risk}})$
\STATE $L \leftarrow \min(2, K_{\max})$ \textbf{if} $\text{prot}$ \textbf{else} $K_{\max}$
\STATE $B \leftarrow [p_h]$; \quad $U \leftarrow C(p_h)$; \quad $Q' \leftarrow []$
\FORALL{$p \in Q[1..]$}
    \STATE $\text{overlap} \leftarrow (U \cap C(p) \neq \emptyset)$
    \IF{$\text{overlap}$}
        \STATE $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow \mathcal{P} \cup \{p\}$ \COMMENT{Promote skipped PR to bound wait}
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $p_{\text{prot}} \leftarrow (p \in \mathcal{P}) \lor (r(p) \ge \theta_{\text{risk}})$
    \IF{$|B| < L \land \neg \text{overlap} \land (p_{\text{prot}} = \text{prot})$}
        \STATE $B.\text{append}(p)$; \quad $U \leftarrow U \cup C(p)$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $Q'.\text{append}(p)$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $Q \leftarrow Q'$; \quad $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow \mathcal{P} \setminus B$
\RETURN $B$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Two-Regime Beta-Binomial Flaky Gating and Cumulative Safety}
Retrying every failed batch indiscriminately wastes runner hours on deterministic regressions, whereas omitting retries causes clean PRs to suffer false rejection whenever a transient flake hits an isolated singleton run. SSMQ estimates suite flakiness online through a conjugate $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)$ posterior initialized to a weakly informative prior $(\alpha_0 = 1, \beta_0 = 19)$ (prior mean $5\%$). Each passing CI run increments $\beta$ by $1$, while any preceding failed attempts on that same job are confirmed transient and increment $\alpha$.

When job $B$ fails on consecutive attempt $m \in \{1, 2\}$, the Beta-Binomial predictive probability of encountering $m$ consecutive transient flakes is:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{flake}}(m \mid \alpha, \beta) = \prod_{k=0}^{m-1} \frac{\alpha + k}{\alpha + \beta + k}.
\label{eq:betabinom}
\end{equation}
Combining $P_{\text{flake}}$ with the batch's prior deterministic failure probability $\hat{q}(B) = 1 - \prod_{i \in B}(1 - \hat{q}_i) \prod_{(i,j)\in \hat{E}(B)}(1 - \hat{p}_c)$ via Bayes' rule yields the posterior probability that the observed failure is transient:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{transient} \mid \text{fail}_m) = \frac{(1 - \hat{q}(B))\, P_{\text{flake}}(m \mid \alpha, \beta)}{\hat{q}(B) + (1 - \hat{q}(B))\, P_{\text{flake}}(m \mid \alpha, \beta)}.
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
Because the cost of misclassifying a flake differs sharply between singletons and multi-PR batches, SSMQ enforces an asymmetric \textbf{two-regime decision threshold} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:flaky}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Singleton Guard ($|B|=1$, $\tau_1 = 0.25$)}: Rejecting a singleton permanently expels the PR from the queue; SSMQ therefore retries singletons whenever $P(\text{transient} \mid \text{fail}_m) \ge 0.25$ (up to $m \le 2$).
    \item \textbf{Batch Guard ($|B|>1$, $\tau_B = 0.50$)}: Splitting a multi-PR batch preserves every clean member without immediate rejection; SSMQ therefore suppresses full-batch reruns unless posterior flakiness reaches at least $50\%$.
\end{itemize}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:flaky} simultaneously enforces \emph{cumulative mainline verification}: if concurrent commits landed on mainline $M$ while job $J$ was executing and introduced a persistent semantic conflict with $J.B$ relative to $J$'s base snapshot $M_0$, SSMQ invalidates the stale pass and re-queues $J$ atop $M$ prior to commit.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cumulative Verification \& Two-Regime Flaky Gate}
\label{alg:flaky}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Completed CI run $R = (J, \text{pass}, M_0, \text{traceback})$, current mainline $M$, posterior $(\alpha, \beta)$
\IF{$R.\text{pass}$}
    \IF{$\text{HasPersistentConflict}(J.B, M \setminus M_0)$}
        \STATE Re-enqueue $J$ at head of pending queue on snapshot $M$
        \RETURN
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $\alpha \leftarrow \alpha + J.\text{failures}$; \quad $\beta \leftarrow \beta + 1$
    \STATE Commit $J.B$ to mainline: $M \leftarrow M \cup J.B$
\ELSE
    \STATE $\tau \leftarrow 0.25$ \textbf{if} $|J.B| = 1$ \textbf{else} $0.50$
    \IF{$J.\text{failures} < 2 \land P(\text{transient} \mid \text{fail}_{J.\text{failures}+1}) \ge \tau$}
        \STATE Re-enqueue retry of $J$ with $J.\text{failures} \leftarrow J.\text{failures} + 1$
    \ELSIF{$|J.B| = 1$}
        \STATE Reject singleton $J.B[0]$
    \ELSIF{$J.\text{allow\_blame}$}
        \STATE Invoke \textsc{CausalBlameAndSplit}($J, R.\text{traceback}$) \COMMENT{Alg.~\ref{alg:blame}}
    \ELSE
        \STATE Bisect $J.B$ prioritizing half containing $J.\text{next\_suspect}$
    \ENDIF
\ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Fail-Safe 1-Step AST/Traceback Causal Blame}
Once a multi-PR batch $B$ is classified as deterministically failing, blind binary bisection requires $\lceil \log_2 |B| \rceil$ sequential CI rounds. SSMQ bypasses blind halving via \texttt{ASTStackCausalRanker} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:blame}), a deterministic static-analysis ranker with zero LLM dependencies. The ranker extracts stack-frame file paths, function symbols, and failing test identifiers from the CI traceback, expands a 1-hop static AST call graph from each failing test, and scores every candidate PR $p \in B$ by intersecting its \emph{AST-modified function definitions}---filtering out comment, docstring, and formatting diffs whose normalized ASTs remain unchanged---against the failing execution cone.

SSMQ then schedules two parallel verification jobs: the clean candidate remainder $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ (with \texttt{allow\_blame=False} and runner-up suspect $s_2$ retained as a bisection priority hint) alongside isolated suspect $\{s_1\}$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single-Culprit Hit}: $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ passes in \textbf{1 recovery round} and commits immediately. Concurrently, if $\{s_1\}$ fails in isolation, it is rejected (subject to singleton flaky gating); if $\{s_1\}$ \emph{passes} alone atop the updated mainline---indicating a rebase-resolvable intra-batch conflict rather than an intrinsic defect---$\{s_1\}$ merges cleanly without developer intervention.
    \item \textbf{Misattribution or Multi-Culprit Fallback}: If $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ still fails, SSMQ transitions deterministically to binary bisection over $B \setminus \{s_1\}$, testing the half containing $s_2$ first. Causal attribution is therefore strictly \emph{fail-safe}: ranking errors can never admit a broken PR or stall progress in an unbounded loop.
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{1-Step AST/Traceback Causal Blame (\textsc{CausalBlameAndSplit})}
\label{alg:blame}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Failing batch $B$ ($|B| \ge 2$), CI traceback $T$, repository base ASTs
\STATE Extract frame files/functions $(\mathcal{F}_T, \mathcal{S}_T)$ and failing test names $\mathcal{T}_F$ from $T$
\STATE Expand 1-hop AST call graph from $\mathcal{T}_F$ to obtain reachable symbols $\mathcal{S}_{\text{reach}}$ and modules $\mathcal{F}_{\text{reach}}$
\FORALL{$p \in B$}
    \STATE $\Delta_{\text{AST}}(p) \leftarrow \text{ChangedFunctionSymbols}(p)$ \COMMENT{Ignores comments/docs}
    \IF{$\Delta_{\text{AST}}(p) = \emptyset$}
        \STATE $\text{score}(p) \leftarrow 0.01$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $\text{score}(p) \leftarrow w_0 + w_f\,\mathbb{I}[F(p)\cap\mathcal{F}_T] + w_r\,\mathbb{I}[F(p)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\text{reach}}]$
        \STATE \qquad $+\sum_{s \in \Delta_{\text{AST}}(p)}\big(w_s\,\mathbb{I}[s\in\mathcal{S}_T] + w_c\,\mathbb{I}[s\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{reach}}]\big)$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $(s_1, s_2) \leftarrow$ Top-2 PRs in $B$ sorted by descending $\text{score}(p)$
\STATE Concurrently enqueue $(B \setminus \{s_1\}, \text{allow\_blame}=\text{False}, \text{hint}=s_2)$ and $(\{s_1\})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Formal Properties and Complexity Analysis}
Three analytical properties govern SSMQ's safety, liveness, and recovery depth.

\begin{proposition}[Cumulative Mainline Safety]
\label{prop:safety}
Under deterministic defect and conflict oracle $G^*$ and Algorithm~\ref{alg:flaky}, neither an individually defective PR nor a PR exhibiting a persistent conflict against previously merged commits can commit to mainline $M$ (zero cumulative escapes).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
Every commit transition $M \leftarrow M \cup J.B$ requires (i) $R.\text{pass}=\text{True}$ against base snapshot $M_0 \subseteq M$, guaranteeing that $J.B$ contains zero intrinsically defective PRs and zero intra-batch or $M_0$-persistent conflicts, and (ii) $\neg\text{HasPersistentConflict}(J.B, M \setminus M_0)$, ensuring that no concurrent commit merged during $J$'s execution window introduced a persistent conflict against $J.B$. By induction on mainline commit steps, $M$ remains free of defects and persistent conflicts.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Starvation Freedom via Protected Promotion]
\label{prop:liveness}
If $N$ PRs precede PR $p$ in ready queue $Q$ upon $p$'s arrival, Algorithm~\ref{alg:packing} dispatches $p$ within at most $N + 1$ batch formations (after at most $N$ preceding dispatches), preventing indefinite overtaking by later-arriving disjoint PRs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
Each invocation of Algorithm~\ref{alg:packing} unconditionally extracts $Q[0]$ (line~1) together with zero or more subsequent PRs while preserving relative arrival order among all remaining entries in $Q'$. Consequently, the number of queued PRs ahead of $p$ decreases by at least $1$ per dispatch, bringing $p$ to $Q[0]$ in at most $N$ dispatches. Once $p = Q[0]$, the next dispatch unconditionally includes $p$ (routing it to the protected lane if $p \in \mathcal{P}$ or $r(p) \ge \theta_{\text{risk}}$), establishing a strict dispatch bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Expected Recovery Rounds and Multi-Culprit Boundary]
\label{prop:complexity}
Consider a failing batch $B$ of size $K \ge 4$ containing $c \ge 1$ culprits, and let $\alpha_1$ denote the Top-1 attribution accuracy of Algorithm~\ref{alg:blame}. When $c=1$, the expected recovery depth to clear the clean remainder is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[R_{\text{SSMQ}} \mid c=1] \le \alpha_1 \cdot 1 + (1 - \alpha_1)\big(1 + \lceil \log_2(K-1) \rceil\big),
\label{eq:blame_rounds}
\end{equation}
which strictly beats binary bisection's $\lceil \log_2 K \rceil$ rounds whenever $\alpha_1 > 1 / \lceil \log_2 K \rceil$ (e.g., $\alpha_1 > 0.33$ at $K=8$). Conversely, whenever $c \ge 2$ simultaneous culprits reside in $B$, evicting a single suspect $s_1$ leaves at least $c-1 \ge 1$ culprits inside $B \setminus \{s_1\}$, forcing deterministic fallback to bisection at depth $1 + \lceil \log_2(K-1) \rceil$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
For $c=1$, Top-1 eviction removes the sole culprit with probability $\alpha_1$, allowing $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ to pass in $1$ CI round; with probability $1 - \alpha_1$, $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ retains the culprit and requires at most $\lceil \log_2(K-1) \rceil$ binary bisection rounds after the failed 1-step split, yielding Eq.~\eqref{eq:blame_rounds}. Setting $\alpha_1 + (1-\alpha_1)(1 + \lceil \log_2(K-1) \rceil) < \lceil \log_2 K \rceil$ and noting $\lceil \log_2(K-1) \rceil \le \lceil \log_2 K \rceil$ gives the sufficient threshold $\alpha_1 > 1/\lceil \log_2 K \rceil$. When $c \ge 2$, $|B \setminus \{s_1\}|$ necessarily contains $\ge c-1 \ge 1$ culprits even when $s_1$ is a true culprit, so $B \setminus \{s_1\}$ fails with certainty and invokes bisection.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:complexity} yields a falsifiable regime prediction that we test directly in Section~\ref{sec:results} (RQ5): 1-step causal blame achieves its largest latency gains in moderate-defect workloads dominated by single-culprit failures, whereas its marginal benefit over bisection vanishes under high defect densities and wide batches where multi-culprit failures predominate.
