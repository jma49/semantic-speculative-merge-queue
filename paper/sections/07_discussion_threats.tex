\section{Interpretation and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\textbf{Membership-partition confounding across architectures.} The simulator models exact-tree authority via set equality ($M_0 \cup J.B = M \cup B_{\text{commit}}$), abstracting order-sensitive patch application and external build state (actual Git trees are checked separately on fixtures). Critically, transient pair conflicts (97\% of sampled conflict pairs) trigger only among PRs placed together in \texttt{job.members}, whereas persistent pairs (3\%) also trigger against \texttt{mainline} and speculative \texttt{prefix}. Consequently, policies that place 1~PR in \texttt{job.members} atop a \texttt{prefix} (\texttt{GitHubMQ-StagedGroup}, \texttt{Aviator-DirAffected}) face systematically fewer transient pair failures than multi-PR-member policies (\texttt{SSMQ*}, \texttt{PRediBatch-RiskGroup}, \texttt{SpeculativeLinear}). Cross-family latency and CI differences in Table~\ref{tab:v8comparators} therefore cannot be interpreted as causal rankings of queue architectures or benchmarks of hosted products. Evaluating conflict-aware ordering inside a stacked train under uniform tree-level pair semantics remains open work.

\textbf{Filtered cones, recall loss, and flaky-gate censoring.} Filtering warm-up mega-PRs ($>20$ files) and directory cliques resolves cone explosion ($D$: \UnfDensity{}\% $\to$ \FcDensity{}\%) and recovers \FcNomGainVsUnf{}~min over unfiltered SSMQ, yet drops realized conflict recall to \FcNomRec{} in plausible/null regimes and leaves 356--382 stale rechecks per run. Thus even sparse unstacked cones trade recall for batch width without overcoming moving-base revalidation. Separately, Algorithm~\ref{alg:flaky} updates $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$ only on confirmed non-stale passes, censoring split, stale, and rejected runs and potentially underestimating flakiness when batches split instead of retrying.

\textbf{Observational and trace external validity.} Our native GitHub Merge Queue audit covers \ObsDays{} inclusive dates across two repositories (\ObsDistinctPrs{} distinct PRs, \ObsAdmissions{} admissions; \ObsTsWfSelected{} selected TypeScript \texttt{merge\_group} workflow runs, distinct from all \ObsTsFetched{} non-draft applicants). Public GraphQL events omit enqueue-time head OIDs and queue-internal batching or culprit logs; all \ObsTsWfDiscordant{}/\ObsTsWfSelected{} TypeScript and \ObsCargoDiscordant{}/\ObsCargoDiscordant{} Cargo \texttt{merge\_group} runs have discordant heads, leaving comparable-hash retries \texttt{unknown} and barring PR-causal regression attribution. In simulation, merged-PR departure gaps, synthetic $12\pm 2$~min Gaussian job durations (shorter than TypeScript's \ObsTsWfP{}~min observed median), decoupled fixed priors, and finite 150-PR burst drain phases constrain generalization; paired bootstrap intervals quantify seed variability conditional on these three traces. Missing proprietary inputs (BMW ML features, Bazel graphs, Uber's conflict analyzer) limit fidelity of literature-inspired comparators.

\textbf{Attribution prototype limits.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:blame} covers synchronous bare-name Python \texttt{FunctionDef} nodes and 1-hop bare calls, excluding methods, \texttt{async} functions, module-level code, and dynamic dispatch, whereas public traces are Go and TypeScript. Both the 13-function Git fixture (serial, no flakiness) and 6 QuixBugs faults (authored \texttt{pass} distractors) isolate mechanism boundaries rather than industrial ranking accuracy.
