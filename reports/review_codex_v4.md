## 2. Summary and key strengths

SSMQ anchors each fresh batch at the ready-queue head, admits additional nonoverlapping PRs within a risk or promotion class, selectively retries failed CI, and checks a suspect singleton and its complement on some failed batches. Three historical file-and-merge-time samples drive a discrete-event simulation with generated faults. A separate authored Python fixture exercises a static suspect ranker on observed unit-test outcomes.

- The comparison worth retaining is to **the evaluated static FIFO policy**. At the nominal point SSMQ delivers 18.18 versus 16.92 PR/h, 81.0 versus 113.8 min mean survivor P95, and 16.75 versus 18.25 runner-min/merge. The paired P95 difference is **−32.81 min** with a bootstrap interval of **[−39.87, −26.12]** for these inputs. The ordering holds in the three samples and reverses at zero injected conflict.
- The new Python stratum genuinely exercises same-file, different-function executable edits. The AST ranker chooses a labeled suspect in 79/80 batches and the complement passes in 79/80. The artifact now executes that intervention and checks the six pair constructions in unit tests. It cleanly establishes feasibility on this controlled fixture.
- The clean graph-construction split and roughly fixed-density sweep improve interpretability. The matched blame ablation is now honestly reported as small and non-significant after adjustment. Those are meaningful advances over v3.

## 3. Blocking and major weaknesses

1. **Blocking for the empirical trace claim and workload provenance.** The purported 200 consecutive merges per repository are demonstrably sampled. In Kubernetes, the first sorted row is #121646 (2023-10-31 18:16:38 UTC), the next is #131085 (2025-05-04), and #121647 and #121649 merged to the same `master` branch seconds after the first yet are absent. In TypeScript, #31116 merged to `master` within the first two stored dates and is absent. Neither an extraction script nor the query, cursors, eligibility rules, or omission counts are in the review snapshot. The sampling mechanism affects the claimed FIFO windows, estimated burstiness, warm-up training, and even the generated truth graph. Chronological sorting reproduces the reported statistics on these files. It cannot substantiate continuity at the repository level. A clean extraction or an independently documented sampling frame followed by recalibration and reanalysis is necessary for the workload conclusions as stated. (`paper/sections/02_background_motivation.tex:14–27`, `data/traces/kubernetes_kubernetes.json`, `data/traces/microsoft_TypeScript.json`, `src/ssmq/loader.py:29–60`.) [Kubernetes #121647](https://github.com/kubernetes/kubernetes/pull/121647), [Kubernetes #121649](https://github.com/kubernetes/kubernetes/pull/121649), [TypeScript #31116](https://github.com/microsoft/TypeScript/pull/31116).
2. **Blocking for the advertised comparison to dependency-aware speculation.** `DepAwareOutcomeTree` really adds tree-per-lane concurrency. A joining PR that overlaps two active lanes is appended to the first while the other continues independently. The implementation neither unifies components nor imposes the cross-lane dependencies promised by the description. The 71.5% P95 advantage identifies performance relative to this particular proxy. Its size is weak evidence about SubmitQueue or dependency-aware speculation generally. A bridge case is easy to construct with lanes for cones `{a}` and `{b}` followed by a PR with cone `{a,b}`. Line 633 assigns it to the first lane, line 635 expands that lane into the second, and lines 637–654 can fund both trees without waiting for the overlap to drain. At commit, only the model's persistent oracle guards cross-lane effects. The two-seed performance smoke test gives no coverage of this invariant. (`src/ssmq/simulator.py:498–686`, `tests/test_ssmq.py:295–306`, `paper/sections/04_evaluation_setup.tex:31–34`.) [SubmitQueue paper, §§4–5](https://lethain.com/static/blog/2019/eurosys19uber.pdf).
3. **Blocking for a general scheduler or integrated attribution contribution.** Reference behaviors and the main drivers are uncalibrated. The scheduler avoids the same cone overlap on which most synthetic conflicts are seeded. The same handcrafted risk score helps generate intrinsic failures and directs the schedulers that use it. The persistent oracle gives every commit path perfect visibility into the modeled interactions at verification or commit. The attribution arm directly draws from true culprit labels and has no live traceback. These are causal design choices within the simulation. They do not empirically establish the graph's predictive power, the ranker's end-to-end value, or the CI safety of exact Git trees. The zero-conflict result and near-density-matched sweep are useful falsification checks within the model. The paper needs at least one independent reference behavior or verified workload for its comparative claims, including one that breaks graph/risk alignment. A simulation paper can meet SIGSOFT standards with a defensible scope, checked mechanics, justified inputs, and validated outputs. The use of simulation itself is an external-validity limit rather than a rejection criterion. (`src/ssmq/simulator.py:102–199,365–377,717–777`, `paper/sections/07_discussion_threats.tex:12–17`.) [SIGSOFT simulation standard](https://www2.sigsoft.org/EmpiricalStandards/docs/standards#simulation).
4. **Major for RQ1 and novelty.** The v3 comment-only weakness is resolved at the demonstration level. The accuracy advantage still depends on a single author-designed program with eleven unique sibling pairings, uneven churn and lexical ties, reused edits and tests, and file and symbol algorithms tuned to that fixture. The results do not establish accuracy on independently authored PRs, wider or more realistic call graphs, or competing edits within the same function. RQ5 provides evidence of a small gain inside this particular scheduler and no evidence of a wide-batch end-to-end regime. The headline novelty is likely the joint policy and its validated operating range. Cone proxies, predictive PR scheduling, noisy bisection, and stack-based ranking have direct prior art.
5. **Submission package blocker independent of science.** The compiled body and final two reference pages meet the numerical 10+2 limit. The submission lacks the after-conclusion **Data Availability** section and access instructions required by the target call, and the IEEEtran float spacing is explicitly compressed. The supplied working checkout carries identifying remote/history metadata. An anonymous supplemental archive or documented alternative is required to verify package readiness. See §8.

External-validity limits that can remain in a properly scoped paper include Python-specific analysis, a finite burst rather than steady-state service, three selected repositories, and simulation controls for otherwise unobservable flake and conflict processes. They become material when the manuscript generalizes beyond those populations or uses universal deployment language.

### RQ2, RQ3 and RQ4

- **Baselines.** Seven primary and six additional ablation strategies match `ALL_STRATEGIES`. Serial uses one worker, static FIFO drains available head batches and recursively halves failures, train jobs validate FIFO prefixes up to depth four, fixed-retry FIFO tries once before splitting, and outcome trees prioritize reach × pass probability × expected PRs per mean CI minute and charge partial cancellations. Both tree proxies use the configured flake rate, conflict probability, persistence share and warm-up priors in their scoring. The depth and cap appear singly selected. Actual SubmitQueue uses target-graph conflict analysis and models dependencies between possible heads, with a different objective and implementation. The lane bridge defect above is separate from these known proxy choices. Compare against tuned static/dynamic, risk-based and dependency-aware designs with validated state transitions. Production GitHub Merge Queue uses staged groups/prefixes with removal/rebuild after failed checks, as documented. Bors documents bisection. GitLab documents prefix trains and restarting downstream pipelines. Grouping these under generic static FIFO or attributing bisection to GitHub misstates the reference systems. (`simulator.py:290–302,387–686,706–744`, `eval/harness.py:173–189,209–258`, `02_background_motivation.tex:4–9`.)
