## 2. Summary and key strengths

SSMQ anchors each new batch at the ready-queue head, restricts additional admissions by locality and risk tier, selectively retries failed verifications, and evaluates a split policy that can check one suspected PR and the remainder separately. The evidence combines three public PR metadata streams, simulated CI, and subprocess execution of authored Python fixtures.

- **The primary static comparison is worth preserving.** At the nominal point, SSMQ delivers 17.78 versus 16.05 PR/h, 79.5 versus 121.9 minutes in mean per-run survivor P95, and 17.05 versus 19.78 runner-minutes per merge. The paired P95 difference is −42.42 minutes with a bootstrap interval of [−49.10, −36.29] within this design. The latency ordering holds in the three reported traces and reverses against static at zero injected conflict. (`paper/generated/tables/main_benchmark.tex:12–17`, `results/results.json:1492`, `paper/generated/tables/graph_recall.tex:12–16`.)
- **The artifact is substantially more inspectable than v1.** It gives a deterministic chronology split, matched fault labels, full main-table intervals for the three primary metrics, per-repository data, a credible within-model safety invariant, and a retry ablation through 15% flakiness. The executable benchmark shows that filtering AST-invariant edits can resolve a specific file-level tie in observed Python test failures.
- **The strongest candidate contribution is the combination and its measured trade-offs.** Co-change heuristics, queue reordering, risk-aware batching, noisy culprit isolation, and AST-based suspicion each have close prior art. The study needs to establish when this particular combination pays off and which mechanisms account for the effect.

### Algorithm 3 and benchmark implementation

- **TeX 117–119.** The benchmark parses Python traceback frames and failing test names, then looks in a hard-wired base fixture and one integration test file. Direct bare-name calls in the failing test are gathered, then their bare-name callees are added. This is one expansion *after* the initial test calls. - **TeX 120–128.** The displayed PR-level score omits implementation terms. ### Propositions 1–3

1. **Cumulative safety, `paper/sections/03_methodology.tex:137–143`.** The induction works for initially clean mainline, fixed unary defects, deterministic symmetric pairwise persistent conflicts, accurate CI over the modeled state (flakes cause false failures only), monotonic mainline growth and an atomic check/commit. On the ordinary SSMQ batch path, passing checks unary and intra-batch faults and old base pairs, while the commit-time delta check covers the other pairs. Model scope matters. The latent oracle is available even at commit time, `_merge` separately counts violations without enforcing its counter as an assertion, and the train and outcome-tree paths use prefix/order invariants instead of this delta check. The zero count across the frozen data verifies consistency of the modeled state transitions. A clean split of a pair marked nonpersistent follows from the injection semantics. The Python fixture executes A and B on copies of a base, so it gives no evidence that a sequential rebase would repair their combined behavior. Exact-tree CI and production green-mainline claims in `paper/sections/02_background_motivation.tex:35–38` and elsewhere require implementation and validation. (`src/ssmq/simulator.py:52–68,299–302,390–412,508–524,548–564,627–639`.)
2. **Dispatch bound, `paper/sections/03_methodology.tex:145–151`.** The \(N+1\) count follows for new arrivals on the append-only ready path, after counting only fresh batch formations. Each such formation removes the head or the target. Head anchoring alone establishes the result. Promotion changes lane and cap. Pending retries, stale checks, bisection and worker saturation can intervene before those formations, and the proof provides neither a time bound nor a merge or acceptance bound. Be explicit about eventual service and finite work assumptions for a liveness claim. (`src/ssmq/simulator.py:270–285,328–368,548–589`.)
3. **Recovery depth, `paper/sections/03_methodology.tex:153–166`.** With independent persistent unary culprits on a fixed base, deterministic observations, no retries or rechecks, and recovery measured as dependency depth under balanced splitting, the single-culprit argument is a valid upper bound. For \(a=\lceil\log_2(K-1)\rceil\) and \(b=\lceil\log_2 K\rceil\), the exact threshold implied by that bound is \(\alpha_1>(1+a-b)/a\). The paper's \(1/b\) is sufficient and may be conservative, for example at \(K=5\). \(b\) is a comparator depth under that model. The proof needs a common endpoint and comparison basis. Actual `mean_blame_rounds` averages maximum recorded depth over faulty recovery trees, including depth-zero singleton failures and adaptive retry depth, without counting stale rechecks as extra rounds. This measures neither CI work nor elapsed recovery time. Total split jobs can be linear and worker contention serializes siblings. A hit can still fail on a flake or a newly persistent base interaction. For multiple **independent** culprits, removing one cannot clear the remainder, but \(1+a\) is a worst-case balanced-tree depth under the stated idealizations, with no guaranteed equality. A batch of eight with two intrinsics where the removed suspect is one and the remaining intrinsic lies in the size-three half of the seven-member remainder illustrates a shallower fallback. If “culprits” includes interacting endpoints, the general claim is false. For edges \((s,x)\) and \((s,y)\) and no unary defects, removing \(s\) clears both interactions from the remainder, even if the simulator initially labels \(x,y\) as culprits. The oracle labels the second member of an interacting pair as a suspect, which is a positional convention. Define “culprit” separately for unary faults, interaction edges, and an edit whose removal makes the batch pass. (`src/ssmq/simulator.py:61–68,526–533,548–619,641–658`.)

## 4. Blocking and major weaknesses

1. **Blocking — the micro-benchmark advantage is entirely the comment-filtering contrast on these fixtures.** There are 12 authored regression mutations and six authored conflict pairs with repeated batch contexts. The nominally 16 co-file cases reuse six module-header comment patches. None changes a sibling function or docstring. Every such comment edit is assigned three added lines against a one-line defect, and churn breaks ties in the file ranker. Churn itself is fixture metadata. The observed split is exactly 16/16 versus 0/16 on co-file comments, 20/24 versus 20/24 on disjoint regressions with the **same four misses**, and 24/24 versus 24/24 on pair cases. Both rankers hit at least one labeled PR in the Top-2 in every case. Hence the 25-point aggregate Top-1 gain establishes a useful behavior on trivia-only changes, with zero observed advantage on the other tested strata. Churn scoring is particularly disadvantaged by the choice of one-line faults and distractor sizes. The baseline uses frame paths and a filename substring heuristic on test names, despite the source header's claim about failing-test imports. A competitive same-information baseline should filter trivia or attribute changed lines to frames, and a controlled ablation should give both methods the same test-to-file evidence. Add held-out, functionally changed co-file siblings, including executed but passing siblings and interacting edits in the same function or file, with balanced churn and position.

   The six pair checks are implemented in unit tests. The sealed benchmark itself executes the full batches, skips those that pass, and scores membership in authored labels. The patcher silently skips unmatched substitutions. The seal does not store raw tracebacks, applied full patch sets, singleton/pair outcomes, or per-batch leave-one-out checks. A failed full batch therefore does not establish that the assigned candidate is necessary in that exact context. The Top-1, Top-2 and MRR labels for pair cases are first-hit metrics, and do not measure recovery on removing a candidate or identification of both edits. The claim that both interacting files occur in the traceback lacks saved evidence and cannot generally hold for assertion-only failures at the test site after callee frames have returned. The 2.247-ms mean times ranking on preloaded strings only; stored individual measurements extend to 3.178 ms. The table's random/churn speed bounds are hard-coded and unmeasured here. (`src/ssmq/blame_bench.py:15–24,182–291,342–379,486–522,539–605`, `tests/test_blame_bench.py:21–57`, `runs/v3_exp0_blame_microbench/metrics.json:1–23`, `eval/audit.py:971–987`, `paper/sections/04_evaluation_setup.tex:13–24`, `paper/sections/05_results.tex:6–11`.)

2. **Blocking — the replay does not validate causal attribution or the proposed regime boundary.** Experiment 8 runs a truth-assisted stochastic rank proxy, and high-defect and low-recall settings change the cap, defect rate and recall together. It records neither distributions of actual root fast-lane batch sizes nor culprit counts/types conditional on failed roots. Mean dispatched job size for full SSMQ is about 1.32 nominally and remains about 1.32 in the 22%/cap-12 setting, including retries and splits. The cap therefore provides no evidence of routinely wide failing batches. The claims “predominantly single-culprit,” “above two,” and “exactly as predicted” cannot be inferred from the sealed data. A difference between significant and non-significant effects does not establish effect modification. The stored P95 gains versus matched NoBlame are 1.45%, 2.48%, 0.49%, and 1.26%, with raw p values 0.03521, 0.001153, 0.74879 and 0.50998. Even a four-P95-test Holm family puts the nominal comparison at about 0.106. A larger prespecified family should reflect the additional tested endpoints. NoBlame P95 differs by only about 1.2 minutes at the nominal point. The “more than 40% across every regime” claim fails at nominal (34.8%) and at several rank-sweep points. The rank sweep varies Top-2 alongside Top-1. The headline 53.6% difference in modeled recovery depth versus static compares entire policies; the matched nominal Full versus NoBlame depth difference is −0.0041 with interval [−0.0273, 0.0203]. (`eval/harness.py:296–349`, `eval/audit.py:659–679,829–849`, `results/results.json:5710,6282,6965–7078`, `paper/sections/01_introduction.tex:19–29`, `paper/sections/05_results.tex:48–57`, `paper/generated/figures/fig_blame_regime.tex:18–30`.)

3. **Blocking — SubmitQueue fidelity and novelty are materially overstated.** Relative to v1, the new baseline really builds branches for pass and fail, tracks path reach probability, and charges partial time for cancellations. The real EuroSys system uses a conflict analyzer and a dependency-aware speculation graph that prunes unnecessary builds and allows independently committable changes. This is central to the comparison because SSMQ's claimed advantage is dependency awareness. The proxy scores FIFO prefix batches, expands a pass/fail tree via halving, resolves through one root, uses synthetic priors and a constant CI duration, and has no independent graph commits or trained/adaptive production model. It even receives configured flakiness and persistence parameters in its score. Treat it as a useful FIFO outcome-tree baseline. The paper's taxonomy marks SubmitQueue as “risk model only” and the introduction says production queues universally avoid dependency analysis. Both contradict the cited work's mechanism. Also compare with prior dynamic batch sizing and risk-aware bisection as live alternatives, or scope the novelty and comparison to the mechanisms actually measured. The serial reference uses one worker, and the fixed-retry baseline changes more than the retry decision relative to SSMQ. Headline results should lead with static FIFO and explicitly name every proxy. (`src/ssmq/simulator.py:243,414–524,535–546`, `paper/sections/01_introduction.tex:4–6,22`, `paper/sections/04_evaluation_setup.tex:33–41`, `paper/sections/06_related_work.tex:14–27`, `paper/sections/07_discussion_threats.tex:7`.) 4. **Major — partial off-graph injection addresses v1's strict circularity while retaining the structural advantage in the generator.** On-graph candidates are still defined by exactly the intersection predicate SSMQ excludes. True defects are drawn from the scheduler's own risk signal. Latent injection adds edges conditional on the realized visible count, is weighted toward same-root pairs, and saturates when the eligible sets run out. At target recall 0.40, the frozen SSMQ runs average 0.467 realized recall (range 0.384–0.611), while average total conflict count rises from about 1,075 at 1.00 to 2,189 at target 0.40. It neither proves the absence of structural bias nor identifies an exact crossover. `SSMQ-NoGraph` removes the protected caps, risk stratification and graph exclusion together. Its result measures the joint batching policy. Pair labels are sampled over all 150 evaluation PRs irrespective of temporal overlap and are permanent when marked persistent. This strongly affects policy-dependent acceptance, with roughly 38/150 rejections by nominal SSMQ, despite an 8% individual defect target and 1.64% reported false rejections. Include total accepted, rejected, and rejection causes beside latency. 6. **Major — paired analysis improved, but interpretation, plots and metric denominators need correction.** The main Wilcoxon tests match by repository and seed and apply Holm across 25 tests. State the normal approximation, handling of zero differences, prespecified families, and assumptions behind signed-rank inference. The 2,000-resample paired bootstrap pools repository–seed differences, while displayed strategy and per-repository intervals use 1,500 marginal resamples. The three fixed repository traces and overlapping experiments provide no population-level independent repository replication. Use language conditional on the evaluated workloads, and stratified matched intervals or a justified resampling unit for stronger claims. Repeated seeds share CI draws only for identical PR membership and attempt index. Cliff's delta uses all cross-sample pairs. The A/A block difference is a descriptive noise check (3.41% throughput, 6.49% P95 and 4.23% cost), without a statistical bound. Lack of significance for false rejection versus static (`p=0.5627`) does not show equivalence.

   P95 and median are averages of per-run quantiles of successful merges, and cost divides runner consumption by successful merges. Throughput includes the queue drain rather than measuring sustainable offered-load capacity. Invalidations include confirmed flaky failures and stale passes as well as speculative branches. The false-rejection numerator counts a rejected PR only if it is nondefective and unblocked against mainline at rejection time. Its denominator counts every intrinsically nondefective input, including ones blocked by persistent interactions, so it is not the complement of clean-PR acceptance. Different merge histories can change who qualifies for the numerator. In the retry sweep, the comparison is useful as a discrete trade-off, but a “Pareto knee” is neither defined nor optimized across a threshold sweep. The 10% figure shows 70.1 min/5.34% for NoFlaky, 96.4/2.23% for SSMQ, and 238.8/0.43% for fixed retry, all for selected strategies and model assumptions. Report survivor latency alongside acceptance and total resolutions. Avoid claiming that lower NoFlaky latency occurs **only** through rejection, since skipping retries also saves service time directly.
