# Independent PC review of Semantic Speculative Merge Queues

**Recommendation — 3/10 (reject for a full ICSE/FSE/EuroSys research paper).** Confidence is high for the artifact and reporting findings.

The paper tackles a practical scheduling problem. The strongest result is the comparison against static FIFO batching at the main operating point, where SSMQ lowers mean per-run P95 merge latency from 78.4 to 49.7 minutes. The replay package and matched defect/conflict labels make that result inspectable. My score reflects three limitations that affect the central contribution:

1. Conflict injection uses the same impact-cone overlap predicate that SSMQ avoids. The graph benefit is interpretable under that model, with no independent evidence about actual pre-merge conflicts.
2. The simulator tests sets of PRs within a job and counts escapes within the subset being merged. The resulting zero count cannot establish safety on the cumulative mainline, and the blame model reads synthetic ground truth to place a culprit at a configured rank.
3. The most dramatic latency comparison is against a restricted SubmitQueue-inspired proxy. The paper also describes a paired experiment but omits paired inference, and its main table promises intervals that it never displays.

I reviewed the manuscript, project rules and plan, generated outputs and reports, manifests, selected stored records, trace snapshots, and evaluation source. I verified hashes and reconciled existing evidence without running the harness, tests, or generators. The writer and reviewer use different model families according to the existing provenance ledger. The same reviewer model previously contributed simulator implementation, which limits independence at the model-family level. A reviewer with no implementation role would strengthen a subsequent pass. The review ledger entry still needs to be recorded by the coordinator under the single-file ownership constraint.

## Gate 3 text-only revisions

- **[BLOCKING] Scope the safety and attribution claims throughout.** In `src/ssmq/simulator.py:217–226,447–455`, pass/fail evaluates job members and the escape counter evaluates the members being merged. There is no cumulative tree state, and the isolation test at `tests/test_ssmq.py:133–145` explicitly expects both endpoints of injected conflicts to be accepted after separate verification. Say the simulator models within-job conflicts, deterministic detection of injected faults, and flakes that cause false failures only. Remove exact-tree guarantees and any claim of exact culprit identification for pairwise faults. In `simulator.py:416–435`, the code selects a true injected culprit and imposes its rank probabilistically. Describe this as simulated attribution with assumed accuracy and cost, including the modeled 0.5-minute overhead on the remainder job. Narrow the title and contribution accordingly.
- **[BLOCKING] Scope the graph result and strongest comparison.** Show that the conflict generator and batch exclusion share the same cone predicate and that the defect-risk weighting also uses the scheduler's features. The NoGraph ablation removes risk lanes, protected batch caps, and disjointness together, so it measures the combined scheduling change. Acknowledge that the risk-gated proxy uses a cumulative linear prefix, a fixed 0.30 risk budget, and at most one root-bisection hedge. The published SubmitQueue planner explores outcome branches using build-value estimates and exploits independence in its speculation graph. Rename the proxy in prose and tables where possible, discuss the fidelity gap, and anchor the abstract on the static-batching comparison. Present GitHub, GitLab, Bors, and Mergify as inspirations when mentioning them, with no vendor performance inference. [Keeping Master Green at Scale, §§4–5](https://www.masoud.io/docs/eurosys19.pdf)
- **[MAJOR] Report actual uncertainty and answer RQ4.** Fix the main-table caption or display existing marginal intervals with that label. Use the per-repository summaries already in `results/results.json` to surface variation, especially the variation in SSMQ's mean P95 across Kubernetes, TypeScript, and Next.js (34.7, 73.0, and 41.5 minutes) and the small TypeScript cost margin against the risk-gated proxy. Throughput, P95, and cost comparisons against static have marginal Cliff's delta values of approximately +0.50, −0.61, and −0.37 respectively. State the direction convention and conditional population. Remove the blanket A/A claim and any significance or universal large-effect claim. SIGSOFT calls for a justified analysis that supports the conclusions. The project also explicitly planned significance testing.
- **[MAJOR] Frame retries as a delivery trade-off and correct metric definitions.** A sealed Kubernetes seed at 1001 records 139 merges and 11 rejections for SSMQ versus 136 merges and 14 rejections for NoFlaky, with the same synthetic ground truth and zero recorded escapes in both. This is a concrete case where disabling retries rejects additional clean PRs. Show the denominator and discuss why cheaper/faster results among accepted PRs can accompany poorer clean-PR acceptance. The comparison with fixed retries changes the whole scheduler, and the sweep excludes NoFlaky. Withdraw the crossover and threshold claims. Describe the Beta update as learning from selectively confirmed transient failures, with missed confirmations after rejected or split jobs. Define blame rounds as maximum recovery depth excluding the initial check, which includes zero-depth singleton failures and can count reruns. Report speculative invalidation as a fraction of builds.
- **[MAJOR] Document the workload, settings, and limits of the artifact.** Surface sampling, trace truncation, merge-time proxies, reuse of seed families, fixed settings, and drain semantics. Label the simulation's CI time distribution and failure rates as assumptions unless their empirical calibration is documented. Distinguish descriptive sensitivity from operational guarantees. Document the external factory dependency, available verification procedure, and manifest discrepancy. The original Gate 2 pass label should carry its actual checker scope.
- **[MINOR] Tighten the analytical and presentation claims.** State the independence assumptions behind the survival product. The number of eligible conflict pairs depends on the overlap graph and can be below the combinatorial maximum. Distinguish balanced bisection depth from total CI work, and remove any implication that a split identifies an interacting pair. Explain that the two blame jobs can run concurrently when slots permit. Use “highest tested flake rate” for the 10% flake point and keep the two tables' formatting consistent through their generation path.

## Follow-up analysis and experiments requiring approval

All new simulations or changes to the frozen harness or metric definitions require human Gate 3 approval, a new tracked sealed `run_id`, and a version bump where applicable. No such work was performed for this review.

1. **[BLOCKING for a production safety claim]** Validate against cumulative repository states in a public shadow merge queue, or explicitly model cumulative versions and test interactions across concurrently validated and sequentially merged batches. Use independently labeled CI failures or an injection scheme that allows conflicts outside the scheduler's graph. Include no-conflict and graph-noise controls, and vary risk correlation independently.
2. **[MAJOR]** Evaluate a faithful or validated SubmitQueue configuration, plus a competitive tuned static policy under the same worker budget. Document variants and tuning protocol, and report occupancy, batch/prefix sizes, clean-PR acceptance, cancellations, and head-of-line blocking. Use a longer replay with backlog monitoring for claims about sustainable throughput.
3. **[MAJOR]** Evaluate an observable ranker with real or independently labeled failure evidence, runtime and error measurements, and matched bisection at each accuracy point. Compare full SSMQ, NoFlaky, and selective/fixed retries across flake rates and thresholds under both independent and correlated failures. Predeclare acceptance and tail latency alongside compute to identify any crossover.
4. **[MAJOR] Existing-record analysis.** Seek an explicit Gate 3 decision before generating revised inferential outputs from the existing seal. Use matched repository–seed contrasts, retaining pairings and accounting for fixed repositories and reused seeds as appropriate. Report effect estimates with intervals and, for Gate 2's prespecified tests, p-values with a stated multiplicity strategy. Publishing existing per-repository summaries needs no new simulation. If accepted, route new numerical outputs through the audited generation path.
